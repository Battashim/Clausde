Level 0 · The very beginning

Start here: what is a coach, a formation, a tactic?

You'll be able to: explain the basic words of football coaching to anyone, even a child.

Like you're 6 · the game in one minute

Two teams. Each team has 11 players. Each team has a goal to protect and a goal to attack. You score when the ball goes fully into the other goal. Only the goalkeeper may touch the ball with his hands, and only inside his big box. A match is 90 minutes: two halves of 45. The team with more goals wins.

What is a coach?

Like you're 6

The players are the musicians. The coach is the conductor. He doesn't play during the match. His job is to get the players ready before the game, help them during the game, and help them learn after the game.

A coach wears many hats. You'll wear all of them:

TeacherTeaches skills and ideas at training. This is the biggest part of the job.
PlannerPlans every session, every week, and the whole season.
SelectorPicks the team, the positions and the substitutes.
TacticianMakes the game plan and changes it during the match.
LeaderMotivates, sets the rules, keeps the group together.
CaretakerProtects health, growth, school and values. With kids, this matters most.
StudentWatches matches, studies, takes courses. A coach never stops learning.

Fàbregas lens

Fàbregas played as a midfielder, the position that sees the most of the pitch. Think like a midfielder: always know where everyone is, even behind you.
  • Coach word: positions

    Four kinds of players

    Goalkeeper stops goals. Defenders protect our goal. Midfielders connect defence and attack and run the most. Forwards score. Shirt numbers often tell you the job: 1 = goalkeeper, 2–5 = defenders, 6 and 8 = midfielders, 10 = the creative player, 7 and 11 = wingers, 9 = the striker.

  • Coach word: formation

    A formation is where players stand

    We count from the back, and never count the goalkeeper. 4-4-2 means 4 defenders, 4 midfielders, 2 forwards. The numbers always add up to 10.

  • Coach word: technique

    Technique is how you do it

    Kicking, passing, controlling, dribbling, heading. Like knowing how to write letters.

  • Coach word: tactic

    Tactic is what you do, when and where

    A plan for beating this opponent: press high or defend deep, play short or long, attack the left or the right. Same formation, different tactic. Like choosing which words to write.

  • Coach word: strategy

    Strategy is the big plan

    Bigger than one match: "this season we build from the back", "in 5 years our academy will produce 3 national team players".

  • Coach word: principle

    A principle is a rule your team always follows

    "When we lose the ball, the nearest 3 press it." Principles stay the same in any formation. Levels 2 and 3 are all about principles.

  • Coach word: offside

    The rule everyone asks about

    When the pass is played, an attacker must not be nearer to the goal than the second-last defender (the goalkeeper usually counts as one). Watch the "Offside" board.

Like you're 6 · putting it together

Technique = how to kick. Position = your job. Formation = where everybody stands. Principle = a team rule we always follow. Tactic = today's plan to beat this team. Strategy = the big dream and the road to it. Coach = the person who teaches, plans and leads all of it.

Try it this week

Watch the first 10 minutes of any match. Write down: both teams' formations (count from the back), one tactic you see (do they press high or wait?), and what the coach does on the touchline.

Level 1 · Coach's eyes

See the game

You'll be able to: watch any match and say what is really happening, not just where the ball is.

Like you're 6

Football is a big game of tag with a ball. When we have the ball, we want the field to feel big, so there's lots of room to run. When they have it, we want the field to feel small, so they have nowhere to go. The game keeps flipping between big and small, again and again.

Fàbregas lens

At Barcelona's academy, La Masia, Fàbregas learned to watch spaces, not the ball. His first question is always: "Where is the free player?"
  • Coach word: the 4 moments

    Every second is one of 4 moments

    1. We have the ball. 2. We just lost it. 3. They have the ball. 4. We just won it. Great coaches have a plan for every moment, not only for attacking.

  • Coach word: thirds, lanes, half-spaces

    The pitch is a map

    Cut it into 3 slices across and 5 streets long-ways. The two "half-spaces" are the streets between the wing and the middle. Defenders get confused there.

  • Coach word: Zone 14

    The most dangerous square

    The area just in front of the penalty box. A player who gets the ball there facing goal can pass, dribble, or shoot.

  • Coach word: off-ball view

    Watch the game, not the ball

    The ball tells you what is happening. The players without the ball tell you what is about to happen.

The 5 questions in a coach's head

Which moment?Do we have it, lose it, defend, or win it?
Who is free?Find the player no one is watching.
How many?Near the ball: are there more of us or more of them?
Where is space?Wide, behind, or between their lines?
What if we lose it now?Who is ready to stop their counter?

Try it this week

Watch 15 minutes of a match with the sound off. Every time the ball changes team, say out loud "Moment 2!" or "Moment 4!". Then watch 5 minutes looking only at one team's back line. Write 3 things you noticed.

      Level 2 · Attacking principles

      Play with the ball

      You'll be able to: explain why the ball moves, and teach players where to stand so they are always free.

      Like you're 6

      You and your friends pass a toy while your little brother tries to grab it. If you all stand in a bunch, he grabs it. If you spread out and always stand where he can't reach, he never gets it. Standing in the right place is called positional play. It's the heart of how Fàbregas thinks.

      Fàbregas lens

      This level is the heart of his football. He grew up doing rondos almost every day at La Masia and learned positional play under Guardiola. Every position has a reason.
      • Coach word: support angles

        Always make triangles

        Never stand in a straight line with the ball and a defender. Make a triangle so the player with the ball has 2 friends to pass to.

      • Coach word: width & depth

        Stretch the pitch

        Some players hold the side lines (width). One stays high behind their defenders (depth). The opponent has to stretch too, and holes appear in the middle.

      • Coach word: superiority

        Be "more" where the ball is

        3 ways to be stronger: more players (3 vs 2), better place (between their lines), or better player (your best dribbler 1 vs 1).

      • Coach word: third man

        A → B → C

        When A can't pass to C, A passes to B and B gives it first-time to C. Defenders watch the ball; the third man arrives free.

      • Coach word: scanning & half-turn

        Look before you get it

        Check over your shoulder before the ball comes. Receive with your body open (side-on) so you can play forward with your first touch.

      Try it this week: the rondo ladder

      Start 4 vs 1 in a 10×10 m square, 2 touches. 10 passes = 1 point. A pass between two defenders (split pass) = 2 points. When it's easy go to 5 vs 2, then 1 touch. Ask: "Where should you stand so the ball can always reach you?" Measure it with your steps →

      3

      Level 3 · Defending principles

      Play without the ball

      You'll be able to: organise a team that is hard to play through and wins the ball back fast.

      Like you're 6

      When they have the ball, our team is one big blanket. If someone pulls one corner, the whole blanket moves, so there are never any holes. And when we lose the ball, we're like bees: the closest bees buzz straight back at it.

      Fàbregas lens

      Fàbregas teams defend forward: a high back line and an instant counter-press, so the opponent has to play in a tiny space far from our goal.
      • Coach word: compactness

        Short and narrow

        Keep about 30–35 metres from your last defender to your striker, and slide as one group toward the ball side. The far side can wait.

      • Coach word: counter-pressing (gegenpressing)

        The 5-second rule

        When we lose the ball, the nearest 3 players attack it at once. The other team is messy right then. If it doesn't work in about 5 seconds, drop back into shape.

      • Coach word: pressing triggers

        Wait for the signal, then jump together

        Triggers: a pass to the side line, a bad touch, a pass backwards, a player facing his own goal. One player pressing alone is useless. Eleven pressing together is a trap.

      • Coach word: cover shadow

        Press with your back

        Run in a curve so your body hides the pass behind you. You press one player and block a second one at the same time.

      • Coach word: block height

        High, middle or low

        Decide where your blanket lives: near their goal (high press), in the middle, or near your own box (low block). Modern teams can do all three.

      Try it this week: 6 v 6 with a bonus

      Normal small game on a 40×30 m pitch. New rule: if you win the ball back within 5 seconds of losing it, that's a bonus point. Count the bonuses. Kids learn to react instead of complaining. Measure it with your steps →

      4

      Level 4 · Fàbregas & Gasperini

      Build your game model

      You'll be able to: describe how your team plays in all 4 moments, and borrow the best of two modern coaches.

      Like you're 6

      A game model is your team's recipe. When everyone knows the recipe, nobody has to stop and think "what do I do now?". Fàbregas's recipe: keep the ball and be clever. Gasperini's recipe: everybody chases a partner and never stops running. Two very different recipes, and both taste great.

      Fàbregas lens

      Fàbregas built his model by mixing his teachers: Barcelona's positional play, Wenger's freedom, Mourinho and Conte's structure. See "The Fàbregas file" below.
      • Coach word: structure vs formation

        A formation is only a photo

        "4-3-3" is where players start. Look at the shape with the ball (often 3-2-5 or 2-3-5) and without it (often 4-4-2). The shape changes; the principles stay.

      • Coach word: positional play (Fàbregas)

        Bait, beat the line, find the free man

        Short passes from the goalkeeper, centre-backs who wait for the striker to come before passing, midfielders who turn between lines, and wingers left 1 vs 1.

      • Coach word: man-oriented pressing (Gasperini)

        Everyone gets a partner

        3-4-2-1 shape. Each player follows one opponent all over the pitch. Centre-backs are allowed to chase into midfield and even join the attack. It needs huge fitness and courage in duels.

      • Coach word: rest defence

        A safety net while attacking

        Keep "their strikers + 1" behind the ball, plus one or two midfielders. Then losing the ball is not a disaster.

      Try it this week

      Watch 20 minutes of a Fàbregas team (Como) and 20 minutes of a Gasperini team (Atalanta 2016–2025, Roma since 2025). For each, write one sentence per moment. You've just written your first two game models.

      Deep dive

      The Fàbregas file

      Like you're 6

      Fàbregas is like a cook who worked in 5 great kitchens. In each kitchen he learned one secret. Now he has his own restaurant (Como), and his recipe mixes all 5 secrets. Tap each kitchen to see what he learned and what you can copy.

      His 8 principles, explained simply

      1 · Goalkeeper first

      The goalkeeper is the first attacker

      Attacks start with the keeper's feet. That gives us one extra player when they press.

      2 · Bait

      Invite the press, then pass past it

      Hold the ball until a defender comes. When he jumps, the space behind him opens.

      3 · More of us

      Be more players behind their first line

      3 + 2 or 2 + 3 at the back against their 2 strikers. Always one free player.

      4 · Fluid roles

      Players move, spaces stay filled

      Full-backs step into midfield, the 8s push forward. Positions change but every important space has someone in it.

      5 · Between lines

      Find the free player between lines

      Midfielders wait in the holes, body open, ready to turn. The third man arrives free.

      6 · Width & 1v1

      Wingers wide, then isolated

      Wingers stay on the side line so they get the ball alone against one defender.

      7 · Defend forward

      High line, instant counter-press

      When we lose it, hunt it right away. The whole team steps up, so the pitch stays small.

      8 · Trust youth

      Young, technical, brave players get chances

      Wenger's lesson. Pick players for their brain and feet, not only their size.

      Train like Fàbregas: 3 sessions to start

      Warm-up · 15 min

      Rondo + hunt

      5 v 2 in a small square. When the 2 defenders win it, they must get it out of the square; the 5 have 5 seconds to hunt it back. Teaches keeping the ball and reacting instantly.

      Main · 20 min

      4 v 4 + 3 magicians

      4 v 4 in the middle, 3 jokers (one at each end, one in the middle) always play for the team with the ball. Point for getting it end to end through the middle joker. Teaches the free man and third-man passes.

      Phase · 20 min

      Build-up against the press

      Goalkeeper + 4 defenders + 2 midfielders against 5 pressers. Score by dribbling over the halfway line. The pressers score in the big goal. Teaches bait, 3 + 2, and courage on the ball.

      Level 5 · Session design

      Train it

      You'll be able to: plan a session and a week that teach your game model through games.

      Like you're 6

      You don't learn to ride a bike by listening to a talk about bikes. You ride, and you wobble, and you get better. Football is the same. Players learn by playing games with special rules that make the lesson happen by itself.

      Fàbregas lens

      In the Fàbregas way, almost every exercise has a ball: rondos, positional games, and build-up against a press. Players get fit by playing, not by running laps.
      • Coach word: constraints-led approach

        Change the rules, change the lesson

        Want more switching play? Put 2 goals on each side. Want faster passing? 2 touches max. Want counter-pressing? Bonus point for winning it back in 5 seconds.

      • Coach word: guided discovery

        Ask, don't tell

        "Where was the free player?" teaches more than "Pass to him!". Players who find the answer themselves remember it in matches.

      • Coach word: freeze, flow, one-to-one

        3 ways to correct

        Freeze everyone (rarely, max 30 seconds), speak while the game flows, or pull one player aside. Most coaching should happen while they keep playing.

      • Coach word: tactical periodization

        Every day has a job

        The week before a game is planned backwards from match day. Small spaces and duels early in the week, big spaces mid-week, short and sharp the day before.

      • Coach word: plan · do · review

        Film yourself

        Put a phone up high on one session a week. Watch it back: did the rules create the lesson? How long did you talk?

      Run the 90-minute session on the right with the rules shown. Afterwards write down: one thing players did better, one thing you said too much, one rule to change next time.

      Level 6 · Mission 1

      Grow Omani players to European level

      You'll be able to: plan what each age group needs, and fix the gaps between a young Omani player and a European academy player.

      Like you're 6

      Growing a footballer is like growing a palm tree. You can't pull it to make it taller. You give it water every day, sunshine, and time, and you give the right thing at the right age. Europe's secret isn't magic. It is lots of good football, every week, for about 10 years, with good coaches the whole way.

      Fàbregas lens

      Arsène Wenger gave Fàbregas his Arsenal debut at 16 and made him captain at 21. The lesson for Oman: trust talented young players early with real responsibility.
      • Coach word: long-term player development

        The right thing at the right age

        Young kids need fun and touches. Pre-teens need skill. Teenagers need understanding and strength. Young adults need hard competition. Skipping a stage leaves a hole that shows later.

      • Coach word: relative age effect

        Don't pick only the big kids

        In almost every country, kids born early in the year get picked more because they're older and bigger, not better. Many late-born or late-growing kids become the best players. Keep them.

      • Coach word: bio-banding

        Group by growth, not only birthday

        Sometimes group players by how grown they are. Small kids get to play with the ball more; early-growers must learn to win with skill, not size.

      • Coach word: deliberate practice

        Touches with a purpose

        Small games (3v3 to 5v5) give each child many more touches and decisions than 11v11. Volume of quality practice is the biggest difference with Europe.

      What a European academy player gets that most kids don't

      Volume

      Training most days

      From about age 12, several sessions a week with a licensed coach, all year.

      Matches

      A real league every weekend

      Competitive games against equal or better teams for about 10 months a year.

      One language

      The same game model from U9 to the first team

      Every coach teaches the same recipe, so players grow up already "speaking" it.

      Support

      School, food, sleep, video

      Football and education fit together, and players review their own games.

      The Oman levers · where you can make the biggest difference

      Lever 1 · Coaches

      Coach the coaches

      The biggest lever. Every age group needs a trained coach using the same model. Run monthly workshops with your staff.

      Lever 2 · Heat

      Train with the climate

      Evening sessions, shade and water breaks, indoor futsal in the hottest months. Futsal also builds close control and fast decisions.

      Lever 3 · Minutes

      More games, all year

      Push for regular leagues in every age group. Add festivals and small-sided tournaments when there's no league.

      Lever 4 · Late bloomers

      Don't cut kids too early

      Check birth months in your squad. Keep late-growers, use bio-banding, judge skill and decisions over size.

      Lever 5 · School

      Partner with schools

      Agree training times and study support, so parents say yes to football.

      Lever 6 · Intensity

      Make training harder than matches

      Duels, speed of play, little standing around. European games are fast every minute; training must be too.

      Lever 7 · Exposure

      Build the level at home first

      FIFA rules limit international transfers under 18, so make home training European-level. Use tournaments, club partnerships and video comparisons with European U-teams.

      Lever 8 · Measure

      Track what matters

      Minutes played, sprint speed, 1v1s won, scanning before receiving, good decisions. Film and compare each season.

      Lever 9 · Mind

      Habits of a pro

      Sleep, food, punctuality, handling mistakes. Talent gets noticed; habits get careers.

      Level 7 · The pathway

      Become the pro

      You'll be able to: plan your licences, your weekly study habits, and how you lead people.

      Like you're 6

      Being a great coach is like being a great teacher and a great captain at the same time. You keep learning every week, you explain things simply, and people trust you because you're fair and you care about them.

      Fàbregas lens

      Fàbregas started small: he coached Como's youth team first, then the first team, while finishing his coaching licences. Start where you are, with the players you have.
      • Coach word: licensing pathway

        Climb the AFC ladder

        In Oman, courses run through the Oman Football Association under the AFC Coaching Convention. Check current entry rules and dates with the OFA before you apply.

      • Coach word: match analysis

        Two matches a week, with a pen

        One top European match and one of your own team. Use the 5 questions from Level 1. Pause, draw, write.

      • Coach word: communication

        Short, clear, kind

        One key message per talk. Talk to players one by one every week. Explain to parents what you're building and why it takes years.

      • Coach word: staff

        Build a small team

        An assistant, a goalkeeper coach, someone for fitness, and someone to film and cut clips. Even volunteers help a lot.

      Level 8 · Mission 3 · The final goal

      Build your own playful coaching style

      You'll be able to: design your own training games, coach in your own way, and explain your method on one card.

      Like you're 6

      Kids don't learn football because a grown-up talks. They learn because they're playing a game they love and the game secretly teaches them. Your job is to be a game inventor. A pirate game can teach pressing. A falcon game can teach when to attack. If they're laughing and trying hard, they're learning.

      Fàbregas lens

      Nobody gave Fàbregas a style. He collected the best ideas from every coach he played for and made them his own. Do the same: borrow from him, then make it yours.
      • Coach word: coaching philosophy

        Your style has 4 layers

        Why: your values (what kind of people you build). What: your game model (Levels 1–4). How: the way you teach (Level 5). Games: your signature activities. Change one layer and your style changes.

      • Coach word: game-based learning

        Hide the lesson inside the game

        Every game has a secret lesson. "Sharks and minnows" secretly teaches dribbling with your head up. "Four goals" secretly teaches switching play. The kids just think they're playing.

      • Coach word: STEP

        4 buttons to make any game easier or harder

        Space (bigger or smaller pitch), Task (the rules), Equipment (balls, goals, cones), People (more attackers or defenders). If it's too easy, they get bored. Too hard, they give up. Aim for "just hard enough".

      • Coach word: themes & stories

        Give every game a story

        Children remember "the falcon dives when the rabbit turns its back" much longer than "press on the trigger". Use stories from their world: falcons, dhows, the desert, superheroes.

      • Coach word: challenge levels

        Make it like a video game

        Level 1: do it with 3 touches. Level 2: 2 touches. Boss level: 1 touch with your weak foot. Players choose their own level and want to "unlock" the next one.

      The 10 fun rules of your sessions

      1. No lines, no queues. If kids are waiting, the game is wrong.
      2. A ball for every player in the warm-up.
      3. Talk less than 30 seconds, then let them play.
      4. Most of the time is playing: aim for at least 70%.
      5. Every game has a score. Give points for what you want to see.
      6. Clap brave mistakes. A failed dribble that was brave gets applause.
      7. Let them choose. Players invent one rule each week.
      8. Name everything. Games, moves and teams get fun names.
      9. Always finish with the big game. It's what they came for.
      10. End with a smile. Ask "What did you enjoy most?"

      Try it this week

      Take one game you already use. Give it a story, a fun name, a scoring system and 3 challenge levels. Run it, then ask the players to invent one new rule. Write down the new version: that's game #1 of your own method.

      Pitch planner

      Every size in this app is in metres, like “20 × 20 m”: 20 metres long and 20 metres wide (400 m² in total). You don't need a tape. Measure your step once, and the app turns every size into a number of steps.

      1 · Measure your stepWalk a distance you know, normally, and count your steps. The app divides the distance by your steps.
      2 · Pace out the gridCone, walk one side, cone, turn 90°, repeat. Then walk both diagonals: if they match, the corners are square.
      3 · Size it to your groupPick the number of players and the type of activity. You get the size in metres and in your steps.

      Foundation 1 · How we teach

      The constraints-led approach

      Research Comes from ecological dynamics, a theory of how people learn movement skills. It has been applied in high-level youth football. Its four key principles:

      Real game informationPractice must contain what players see in matches: opponents, team-mates, goals and direction. Coaches call this representative learning design.
      Change the conditionsChange the space, the rules, the equipment or the players, and the problem changes. Players find the solution themselves.
      Repetition without repetitionRepeat the same idea in slightly different situations each time, instead of the same drill again and again.
      Outside focusPoint attention at the outcome ("get it to the free side") rather than body parts ("bend your ankle").

      Football principle The 4 STEP buttons from Level 8 (Space, Task, Equipment, People) are exactly these constraints. Our design The lab below turns them into ready-made fixes.

      Foundation 2 · Our main game for ages 6–12

      Funino: 3 v 3 on four goals

      Research Created by the German coach Horst Wein to develop game intelligence in children.

      • Rules 3 v 3, no goalkeepers. Each team attacks two mini-goals, placed far apart on the end line.
      • Rules Goals count only from inside the shooting zone, usually about 6 m from the end line (shorter for the youngest).
      • Federation Germany's federation (DFB) made new children's formats based on Funino compulsory for U6–U11 from the 2024/25 season.
      • Why it works Few players means many touches. Two goals means a real decision every attack: which goal is open?

      Foundation 3 · A habit we measure

      Scanning: look before you receive

      • Research Norwegian researcher Geir Jordet studied Premier League midfielders and forwards (2013, 118 players, 1,279 game situations). Players who scanned more completed more passes.
      • Research Top scanners looked around about 0.6 times per second before receiving (Lampard 0.62, Gerrard 0.61).
      • Research A later study (2020) found scanning has a small but positive effect on performance. It helps; it is not magic.
      • Football principle A scan is a quick head movement to look away from the ball, to see team-mates, opponents and space. It happens before the ball arrives.
